# GIDA Labs — Narration Recording Script (v6)

**Purpose:** Record authentic educator narration for the GIDA accessibility experience. The script is for the teacher's own voice and can be used to replace browser text-to-speech for selected prompts. The assessment itself should remain accessible through text-to-speech for newly uploaded materials.

## Recording instructions

Record each numbered clip separately as WAV or MP3 in a quiet room. Speak naturally at a measured pace; leave about one second of silence at the beginning and end. Use descriptive filenames such as `01_welcome.mp3`, `04_sophia_question.mp3`. Do not speak any student's identifying information from actual records. The names below are existing illustrative case-file names in the GIDA platform. Avoid giving correct answers during question prompts. Record answer options separately when possible so the learner controls when they are read.

### 01 — Welcome and purpose

“Welcome to GIDA Labs: Assessment Investigation. Our guiding question is this: When does assessment measure what a student knows, and when does it measure whether the student can access the assessment? As you investigate, remember that one score is only one piece of evidence. We will consider language, visual access, communication mode, and the way a question is presented. Our goal is not to make a task easier. Our goal is to understand what the assessment is actually measuring.”

### 02 — Instructions for all case files

“Choose a case file. Review the student's access conditions, then complete the assessment as presented. Notice which features help you understand the question and which may interfere. After you respond, select Analyze Evidence. An incorrect response is a starting point for investigation, not a diagnosis.”

### 03 — Visual-access case introduction: Sophia

“You are opening Sophia's case file. This investigation intentionally changes the visual presentation of a question. It is not a simulation of blindness, low vision, dyslexia, or a specific disability. It demonstrates how presentation barriers can interfere with the evidence an assessment produces. You may choose audio access to hear the prompt and the available response options. Think about whether the scientific learning target changes when the mode of access changes.”

### 04 — Sophia: assessment question (Spanish)

“¿Qué necesitan las plantas para crecer?”

### 05 — Sophia: response choices (record as separate clips if possible)

“Agua.”

“Luz solar.”

“Rocas.”

“Aire.”

“Dulces.”

### 06 — Audio accommodation explanation

“In the classroom investigation that informed GIDA, a student with a visual impairment was provided audio access to assessment content. The accommodation allowed the student to hear material that would otherwise have depended on visual reading. The purpose was to gather clearer evidence of content understanding. Audio access should be documented as an assessment condition, rather than treated as a change to the learning target.”

### 07 — Before reviewing a result

“Consider what the student demonstrated. Was the response independent? Was the question read aloud? Were the choices repeated? Did the student use a picture, a spoken answer, a gesture, or an assistive communication system? Record these conditions before interpreting the score.”

### 08 — After reviewing a result

“A correct response can support a claim of understanding, but one item does not establish mastery. An incorrect response can reflect a content gap, an access barrier, or both. Compare evidence across attempts and conditions. Ask what the student can demonstrate when the assessment is accessible.”

### 09 — Student portfolio introduction

“Student portfolios show patterns across time. Review more than one work sample, language sample, or assessment attempt. Look for changes in performance when the task, language, or mode of response changes. Avoid drawing a high-stakes conclusion from a single score.”

### 10 — Curriculum and rubric studio

“GIDA allows educators to build assessments for different curricula and grade levels. When creating a rubric, describe observable evidence of learning. Keep the academic target separate from language or sensory access requirements unless those requirements are themselves part of the target. When recording results, note accommodations and the conditions under which the student responded.”

### 11 — Self-assessment introduction

“Student self-assessment adds another source of evidence. Invite learners to explain what they understood, what felt difficult, and what support helped. Young learners may communicate their reflections through pictures, pointing, speech, or other accessible response methods.”

### 12 — Professional consultation introduction

“Professional consultations connect GIDA's classroom investigations with research on multilingual learning, assessment validity, and access. These perspectives help us interpret evidence cautiously. They do not replace individualized observation, repeated assessment, or professional judgment.”

### 13 — Closing reflection

“The central question remains: Are we measuring the learning target, or are we also measuring access to the task? A fair assessment does not remove rigor. It creates conditions in which a learner has a meaningful opportunity to demonstrate knowledge. Document the evidence, identify what is still uncertain, and choose the next assessment deliberately.”

## Using recordings in a future version

Send the numbered audio files together in a ZIP. The clips can be mapped to the matching GIDA screens and questions, with a **Play recorded narration** button and browser text-to-speech as a fallback. For uploaded teacher-created assessments, the general text-to-speech engine will still be necessary unless matching audio is also supplied. Narration should not read correct answers or interpretation feedback before the learner submits their response.

## Voice cloning option

A synthetic voice based on your own recordings is technically possible through third-party voice services that support consent-based voice creation. It requires a service, a suitable recording sample, and careful review of its privacy and licensing terms. This version does **not** clone a voice or send recordings to any external service. Using your own prerecorded clips is the simpler, more controlled starting point.
